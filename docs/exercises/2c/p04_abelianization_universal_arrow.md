# 2C·P04 — Abelianization as a universal arrow

**Type:** core · **Technique:** existence and uniqueness of a factorisation · **Unlocked by:** `2c_universal_arrows` §3, §6
**Source:** adapted from Leinster §2.1 and §2.3

## Statement

Let $U \colon \mathbf{Ab} \to \mathbf{Grp}$ be the inclusion functor. For a group $H$, let
$[H, H]$ be the subgroup of $H$ generated by all commutators $xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$ ($x, y \in H$).
You may assume that $[H, H]$ is a normal subgroup of $H$. Write $H^{\mathrm{ab}} = H/[H, H]$
and $\pi_H \colon H \to H^{\mathrm{ab}}$ for the quotient homomorphism.

1. Prove that $H^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is abelian, so that $(H^{\mathrm{ab}}, \pi_H)$ is an object of
   the comma category $(H \Rightarrow U)$.
2. Prove that $(H^{\mathrm{ab}}, \pi_H)$ is an initial object of $(H \Rightarrow U)$.
3. Deduce that $U$ has a left adjoint, and say what the left adjoint does to a group
   homomorphism $p \colon H' \to H$ (a description as "the unique homomorphism such that…" is
   what is wanted).

## What you may use

- Standard facts about quotient groups: $\pi_H$ is a surjective homomorphism with kernel
  $[H, H]$; and if $\phi \colon H \to K$ is a homomorphism whose kernel contains a normal
  subgroup $N$, then there is a unique homomorphism $\bar\phi \colon H/N \to K$ with
  $\bar\phi \circ \pi = \phi$, where $\pi \colon H \to H/N$ is the quotient map.
- The definition of $(A \Rightarrow G)$ and of a universal arrow (2C §3).
- Corollary 4 of the lecture, and the construction in its proof.

## What a complete proof must show

- [ ] Part 1: for arbitrary elements of $H^{\mathrm{ab}}$, written as cosets, the two products are shown equal.
- [ ] Part 2 states what must be proved in elementary terms: for every abelian group $K$ and homomorphism $\phi \colon H \to K$, there is exactly one homomorphism $q \colon H^{\mathrm{ab}} \to K$ with $q \circ \pi_H = \phi$.
- [ ] The hypothesis needed to apply the quotient fact is verified, and it is said where "$K$ is abelian" is used.
- [ ] Existence and uniqueness of $q$ are both addressed.
- [ ] Part 3 cites the result used and gives the defining equation of the left adjoint on arrows.
